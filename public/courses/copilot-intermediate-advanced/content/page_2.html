Of all these, instructions and skills are most often confused, so remember this:

Instructions are the behaviour, skills are the format

Instructions say who the agent is and what it must never do.

Skills say how one output is formatted. The NSS agent’s skill sets out the ten report sections in order, the columns of the themes table, and how the flagged comments are laid out.

The NSS agent’s reports have the right themes and counts, but the sections come back in a different order from the house template each time. Where does the fix belong?

Knowledge, tools, and grounding

An agent is grounded when its answers come from sources you chose: your documents and your University’s own material, rather than whatever the model knows in general. A grounded answer is about this specific programme, not about programmes like it.

The NSS agent is grounded in three documents, each with a job:

  • This year’s workbook is the evidence. Every number and quote comes from it.

  • The report template shows what the finished report looks like.

  • Last year’s signed report gives the comparison, an example of the University’s own style.
